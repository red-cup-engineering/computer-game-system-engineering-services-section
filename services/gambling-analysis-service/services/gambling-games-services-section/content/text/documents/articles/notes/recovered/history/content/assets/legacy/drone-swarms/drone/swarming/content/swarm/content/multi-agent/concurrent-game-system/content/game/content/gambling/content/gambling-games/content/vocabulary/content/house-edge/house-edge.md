The house edge is the mathematical advantage built into a [gambling game](../index.md) for the operator.
It represents the difference between the true [odds](../../terms/odds.md) of an event and the odds at which the operator pays out, ensuring that the operator retains a percentage of all [wagers](../../terms/wager.md) over time.

House edge is typically expressed as a percentage of each wager that the operator expects to keep on average.
A game with a 5% house edge returns 95 cents of every dollar wagered in the long run.
Different games carry different edges: [blackjack](../blackjack/index.md) with strong player decisions under favorable rules can have an edge under 1%, roulette with a double-zero wheel has 5.26%, and slot machines vary widely but often exceed 5%.
Within a single game, different wager types can carry different edges - in craps, the pass line bet has a 1.41% edge while proposition bets can exceed 10%.
In blackjack, rule changes such as 6:5 blackjack payouts or the dealer hitting soft 17 increase the edge even when the visible gameplay looks the same.

The house edge is a long-run statistical expectation, not a guarantee for any individual session.
A player can win in the short term despite a negative expected value, and the operator can lose on any given day.
What the edge ensures is that across thousands of wagers, the operator's revenue converges predictably on the edge percentage multiplied by total action.
This predictability is what makes gambling a viable business and is the reason that casinos, bookmakers, and lottery operators can offer large individual payouts while remaining profitable.
