A hand in [poker](../index.md) is a set of cards held by or assigned to a player, used to determine the outcome of a round [@britannica2026poker].
The term has two related meanings: the specific cards a player holds and a complete round of play from deal to [showdown](./showdown.md).

Poker hands are ranked by a hierarchy that reflects the probability of being dealt each combination from a standard [deck](../../../../card-games/terms/deck.md).
The rarest combinations rank highest: a royal flush is the strongest hand in most variants, while a high card is the weakest.
Between these extremes, the standard ranking runs through pairs, two pair, three of a kind, straights, flushes, full houses, four of a kind, and straight flushes.
Some poker variants modify this hierarchy or introduce additional categories.

The strength of a hand is always relative to what opponents hold, which is unknown until showdown.
This incomplete information is what makes poker a game of skill rather than a pure lottery: players must infer the likely strength of opposing hands from betting patterns, position, and revealed community cards [@sklansky1999].
A hand that is statistically strong can still lose, and a weak hand can win the [pot](./pot.md) if opponents [fold](./fold.md) to a well-timed bet.

## Related terms

- [pot](./pot.md) - The hand is what players are trying to turn into a claim on the pot.
- [showdown](./showdown.md) - At showdown, the remaining hands are compared directly.
- [fold](./fold.md) - Folding ends a player's use of the hand without requiring its strength to be revealed.
