To double down in [blackjack](../../../../../../README.md) is to double the original [wager](../../../../../../content/text/glossary/terms/wager.md) in exchange for committing to receive exactly one additional card [@thorp1966]. The action increases both risk and reward: the player invests more precisely when the expected value of one more card is favorable enough to justify the larger stake.

Double-down rules vary by ruleset. Some tables allow doubling on any first two cards. Others restrict it to totals such as 9, 10, or 11, or prohibit it after a [split](./split.md). Because doubling changes how much money enters favorable situations, its availability materially affects the [house edge](../../../../../gambling-games-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/house-edge.md) [@shackleford2025blackjackcalculator].

## Related terms

- [split](./split.md) - Some rulesets permit doubling after a split, while others do not.
- [soft hand](./soft-hand.md) - Certain soft totals are classic doubling situations under favorable rules.
- [house edge](../../../../../gambling-games-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/house-edge.md) - Doubling rules change how much value the player can capture from strong positions.
