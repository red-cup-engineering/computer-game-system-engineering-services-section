To surrender in [blackjack](../../../../../../README.md) is to concede the hand before play is completed and lose only part of the original wager, usually one half [@thorp1966]. Surrender exists because some starting positions are weak enough that cutting the loss is better than continuing.

Not all blackjack tables offer surrender, and the timing matters. Late surrender, where the option is available only after the dealer checks for a [natural blackjack](./natural-blackjack.md), is more favorable to the house than early surrender. Because surrender gives the player a controlled way to cap losses in especially weak positions, its presence lowers the [house edge](../../../../../gambling-games-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/house-edge.md) [@shackleford2025blackjackcalculator].

## Related terms

- [natural blackjack](./natural-blackjack.md) - Dealer blackjack checks determine when late surrender is available.
- [hard hand](./hard-hand.md) - Surrender is most often used on especially weak hard totals against strong dealer upcards.
- [house edge](../../../../../gambling-games-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/house-edge.md) - Surrender rules change the player's ability to limit losses and therefore affect the edge.
