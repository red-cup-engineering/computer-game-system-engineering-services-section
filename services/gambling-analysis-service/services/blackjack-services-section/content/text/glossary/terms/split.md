To split in [blackjack](../../../../../../README.md) is to divide a starting pair into two separate hands, each matched by an additional wager equal to the original bet [@thorp1966]. If the player is dealt two 8s, for example, they may split them and play each 8 as the base of a new hand.

Splitting matters because it can turn one weak composite hand into two stronger strategic positions. A pair of 8s totals 16, which is awkward as a single [hard hand](./hard-hand.md), but splitting creates two fresh 8-start hands with new decision paths. Rules about whether players may resplit, split aces, or double after splitting materially change blackjack strategy and expected value [@shackleford2025blackjackcalculator].

## Related terms

- [double down](./double-down.md) - Whether a player may double after splitting is a major rule variation.
- [hard hand](./hard-hand.md) - Splitting often breaks up an awkward hard total into two more playable starting hands.
- [house edge](../../../../../gambling-games-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/house-edge.md) - Split rules affect expected value and therefore the house edge.
